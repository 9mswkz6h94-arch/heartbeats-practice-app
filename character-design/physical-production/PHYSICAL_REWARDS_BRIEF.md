## Recommendation

Start with a 3-inch circular, iron-on or sew-on embroidered patch as the real-world version of a teacher-created badge. Keep the digital award and physical purchase as two separate actions:

1. The teacher recognizes the student in Heart Beats.
2. A guardian chooses whether to request or purchase the keepsake.
3. The guardian provides the shipping address directly in the fulfillment flow.

Do not send a child’s name, account details, or home address to a vendor from the teacher workspace. A physical item should use an order reference and optional guardian-entered first name only.

## Badge vendor fit

### Recommended pilot: Printful embroidered patches

- Circular patches are available at 3 inches with iron-on, sew-on, safety-pin, or hook-and-loop options.
- Printful says there is no minimum quantity, orders are fulfilled on demand, and it ships worldwide.
- The current published starting price is $8.25 per standard patch, plus a one-time digitization fee for a new design; final pricing and shipping must be rechecked before a pilot.
- Printful also documents a group-order link in which each recipient supplies their own delivery address. A guardian-owned link is a better privacy shape than collecting addresses in a student profile.
- Use the iron-on or sew-on version for the child-facing pilot. Order and inspect one sample before offering it to families.

Source: https://www.printful.com/custom-patches

### Not the default child wearable: pinback buttons

Printify can print and ship individual metal pin buttons with no inventory, but the product uses a steel safety-pin backing. Printful’s current pin-button listing is made on demand but explicitly lists an adult age restriction and sells the product in sets of five. That makes pinbacks a possible teacher/family display item, not the default student wearable.

Sources:

- https://printify.com/app/products/430/generic-brand/custom-pin-buttons
- https://www.printful.com/custom/pins/personalized/set-of-pin-buttons

## Artwork package for a patch pilot

Build one simplified badge mark rather than sending the full sticker illustration directly to embroidery:

- 3-inch circular composition
- one character head-and-instrument silhouette
- badge title omitted from the art so one design can support many teacher messages
- no gradients, glow, shadows, or tiny facial details
- thick shapes and generous negative space
- maximum 15 thread colors; prefer 6–8
- transparent PNG and vector PDF master
- safe margin based on the final vendor template
- one physical sample checked for expression, thread color, edge quality, wash/attachment behavior, and legibility at arm’s length

## Plush feasibility

Plush is achievable, but it is a separate product-development project rather than another image export. The character’s instrument anatomy, silhouette, seams, embroidery, fabrics, balance, age grading, labels, packaging, testing, and production sample all have to be resolved in three dimensions.

### Lowest inventory risk: Makeship campaign

Makeship’s creator program covers prototype design, production, fulfillment, customer service, and campaign support. Its standard model uses a 21-day preorder campaign and requires 200 supporters before production begins. Published manufacturing time can be up to about 90 days after a successful campaign. This is the best route if the studio can realistically gather that audience and is comfortable with a limited “you had to be there” drop.

Source: https://marketing.makeship.com/creators

### Smaller controlled batch: Budsies / Stuffed Animal Pros

Budsies publishes a 50-piece minimum for a repeated custom design and a $349 bulk sample requirement. Its separate campaign program publishes a 100-piece guarantee. This is a useful backup when 200 preorders are too ambitious but the studio can fund or sell a small batch.

Sources:

- https://support.budsies.com/support/solutions/articles/5000593822-do-you-take-bulk-orders-
- https://www.budsies.com/market-faq/

### Traditional wholesale scale: CustomPlush.com

This route currently publishes a 500-piece production minimum, prototype fees starting at $500, and an estimated 90–120 days after prototype approval. It is more appropriate after a character has demonstrated demand.

Sources:

- https://customplush.com/faq/
- https://customplush.com/how-it-works/

## Child-product safety gate

This is product planning, not legal advice. Before offering a plush as a product for children in the United States, identify who is the domestic manufacturer or importer and get a written compliance plan. Current CPSC guidance says children’s toys must comply with the mandatory ASTM F963 toy standard. Products intended primarily for children 12 or younger may require testing by a CPSC-accepted third-party laboratory, a Children’s Product Certificate, and permanent tracking information on the product and packaging. As of July 8, 2026, CPSC also requires importers of most regulated consumer products to electronically file certificate data at import.

Design the first prototype conservatively:

- embroidered eyes and facial details
- no detachable accessory, hard eye, magnet, battery, sound module, wire, or exposed fastener
- instrument forms integrated into the soft body
- short, reinforced appendages that survive pull testing
- documented fabric, thread, stuffing, dyes, and production batch
- sewn-in tracking/identity label and age grading reviewed before production

Primary guidance:

- https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Toy-Safety
- https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/Childrens-Product-Certificate
- https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/tracking-label
- https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Toy-Safety/ASTM-F-963-Chart

## Suggested sequence

1. Finish the digital Badge Studio behavior and approve its language.
2. Convert one character into the simplified embroidery badge mark.
3. Order one studio-addressed Printful patch sample; do not use student data.
4. Inspect cost, color, attachment, and actual finish before designing a guardian request flow.
5. For plush, choose one lead character and create front/side/back turnarounds plus a seam-and-material callout sheet.
6. Commission one prototype. Do not open preorders until the physical prototype and compliance responsibilities are approved.
7. Choose Makeship if a 200-order campaign is realistic; otherwise price a 50-piece Budsies pilot.

Riffin and Chordillo are the strongest first plush candidates because their silhouettes and instrument ideas remain recognizable when simplified. Riffin is likely the friendlier first choice; the curved guitar-neck tail will need careful reinforcement and should stay soft, short, and inseparable from the body.
